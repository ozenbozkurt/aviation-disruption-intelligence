Key operational insight

9 September ranked as the broadest disruption day (94.8/100) because elevated ATFM delay coincided with weak arrival and departure punctuality. 16 September had the month's highest ATFM delay per flight (1.688 min/flight) but a lower combined disruption score. One metric alone would miss that distinction.

What I would investigate next

  1. Weather and convective activity on 9 and 16 September.
  2. ATC capacity restrictions and network regulations.
  3. Airport capacity, infrastructure constraints or industrial action.
  4. Delay-cause categories and affected airports / flows.
  5. Whether signals were visible early enough for staffing and disruption-management decisions.

The dashboard intentionally stops at evidence. Cause requires additional data.

Transparent methodology

The index ranks days relative to this 30-day period. It is designed for screening and explanation, not as an absolute operational benchmark.

Analyst principle

Measurement first. Causal hypothesis second.

High traffic is displayed as context but is not scored. A busy day is not automatically a disrupted day.
